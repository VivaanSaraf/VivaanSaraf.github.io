What is VEX IQ?

VEX IQ uses parts that fit together, motors, sensors, and code to build small robots. In a competition, teams make a robot for a game played on a field. Its wheels and driving motors are called the drivetrain; an intake brings game pieces into the robot; and a flywheel is a fast-spinning part. Code tells these parts when to work.

My build

From code to competition

I started working on VEX IQ in 2024 and continued in 2025. Before our team could take a robot onto the competition field, it needed to move around and handle the game pieces. Our robot used two motors to drive its wheels, an intake to collect pieces, and a flywheel that could spin quickly.

After the mechanical parts were in place, the next challenge was making them respond to the right inputs. In my saved VEXcode Blocks project, I used controller input and a bumper sensor to control the flywheel. The code had to work with the real motors and sensors, not just look correct on the computer.

We practiced and tested the robot on the field as a team. Watching how it actually moved and handled game pieces helped me understand how mechanical design and programming affect each other. That hands-on testing was an important part of getting ready for competition.

Result: In my second year of VEX IQ, our team placed 2nd at Nationals. My documented programming work includes the flywheel controls; building and testing the competition robot was a team effort.
