SO-101 Robot Arm

What is a robot arm?

A robot arm has motor-powered joints, a little like a human arm, and a gripper that can hold objects. In the SO-101 setup, moving the “leader” arm by hand can make the “follower” arm copy its motion. Calibration matches the positions of the two arms; teleoperation means using the leader to control the follower.

My work

Getting the arms ready

I am setting up two SO-101 arms so that one can follow the movement of the other. Before I can try that, I first need to configure the arms on my Mac. The computer has to find the connection for each arm, and I need to check that their motors respond.

I am also working with calibration files, which help the software understand each motor's position, and setting up cameras for the LeRobot software. These steps matter because the follower cannot copy a movement reliably if the connections or motor positions are wrong.

As each part of the setup is ready, I can test teleoperation in small steps: moving the leader arm by hand and checking whether the follower copies it. I am still working through the hardware and software, and each check brings me closer to getting the two arms to move together.

Current stage: I am checking connections, calibration, cameras, and small leader-to-follower movements. The full setup is still in progress.
